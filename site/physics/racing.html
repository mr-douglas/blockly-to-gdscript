Give the racing cars real physics

Objective. Replace the racing cars’ instant movement with forces, so they accelerate, carry momentum, slide on corners and bounce off walls and each other.

Start from the finished racer

This script extends the last lesson of Build a Two-Player Racer, so the track, shared camera, gates and laps are already there. It replaces just one function: drive(). The old version set the velocity straight to throttle * SPEED every frame. That means the cars reach full speed instantly, stop instantly and forget how fast they were going.

car.velocity = -car.transform.basis.z * throttle * SPEED

Forces, then acceleration, then velocity

The new drive() never sets the velocity directly. It adds up the forces on the car, divides by the mass to get the acceleration (Newton’s second law, a = F / m), and adds that to the velocity a little every frame. Because the velocity is kept between frames, the car keeps moving when you let go. That is momentum.

force += forward * ENGINE_FORCE * throttle
force -= car.velocity * (DRAG * speed + ROLLING)
var acceleration := force / MASS
car.velocity += acceleration * delta

Air DRAG grows with speed × speed. Eventually it balances the engine and the car stops speeding up. That is its top speed. ROLLING resistance makes a coasting car slow down and stop.

Grip, steering and collisions

  • Grip. Tyres resist sliding sideways. Each frame the code removes part of the sideways velocity. With less GRIP the car drifts wide on corners.
  • Steering. The turn is scaled by the forward speed, so a stopped car cannot spin on the spot and a reversing car steers the other way.
  • Walls. bounce_off_wall() reverses only the part of the velocity going into the wall and shrinks it by WALL_BOUNCE. Glancing blows keep most of their speed.
  • Car bumps. bump() gives both cars equal and opposite pushes, so their total momentum is the same before and after. A fast car rams a slow one forward and slows down itself.

Predict, change, and test

  1. Hold Up for a few seconds and watch the speedometer. Predict the top speed, then halve DRAG and test again.
  2. Double MASS. What changes: the top speed, the acceleration, or both?
  3. Set GRIP to 1.0 and take a corner at full speed. Then try 30.0.
  4. Set WALL_BOUNCE to 1.0 and drive into a barrier at an angle. Then try 0.0.
  5. Park Green on the track and ram it with Orange. Which car ends up faster?
Check the prediction

Top speed is where the engine force equals drag plus rolling resistance. With the starting values that is about 6.8 m/s, and halving DRAG raises it to about 9.1 m/s. Doubling MASS halves the acceleration, so the car takes longer to get going and longer to stop. The top speed stays the same, because mass is not in the balance of forces. Low grip makes the car slide like it is on ice, and very high grip makes it feel like it is on rails. A wall bounce of 1.0 makes a perfect bounce, and 0.0 kills all speed into the wall. With CAR_BOUNCE at 0.6, Orange keeps only a fifth of its speed and Green gets the other four fifths, so Green ends up faster.

Success criteria. The cars speed up and slow down smoothly, slide on fast corners, bounce off barriers and push each other. You can explain each change using force, mass, acceleration and momentum.

Apply it to your project. Give each car its own MASS and ENGINE_FORCE to make a heavy truck and a light kart, then race them. Godot also has a ready-made VehicleBody3D node with suspension and wheels for when you want even more realism.
