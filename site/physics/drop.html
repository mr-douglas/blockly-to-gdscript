Blockly to GDScript

Physics course · Lesson 1

Drop a crate onto the ground

Objective. Make a body that the physics engine moves for you, and start it at a random angle.

Let the engine do the moving

A CharacterBody2D only moves when your code calls move_and_slide(). A RigidBody2D is different: once it is in the scene, Godot applies gravity, works out collisions and makes it bounce, slide and tip over. There is no _physics_process in this lesson at all.

Every physics body needs a collision shape. add_box() gives the crate a 70 × 70 RectangleShape2D, and block_art() draws the Kenney wooden crate on top at the same size.

crate = RigidBody2D.new()
crate.position = Vector2(384, 60)
crate.rotation = randf_range(0, TAU)
add_box(crate, CRATE_SIZE)
block_art(crate, CRATE_SIZE, "wood")
add_child(crate)

Rotation in Godot is measured in radians. A full turn is TAU (about 6.28) radians, which is 360 degrees. randf_range(0, TAU) picks a different starting angle every time the scene starts, so press R a few times and watch the crate land differently.

Predict, change, and test

  1. Replace the random angle with crate.rotation = 0. Predict how it lands, then test it.
  2. Try crate.rotation = TAU / 8 (45 degrees). Does it always fall to the same side?
  3. Change the height in crate.position to 250. Does a lower drop make the crate bounce less?
  4. Change CRATE_SIZE to Vector2(140, 70). Why does the picture change to a plank?
Check the prediction

With a rotation of 0 the crate lands flat and stays still. At exactly 45 degrees it lands on a corner. In a perfect world it would balance, but tiny rounding errors in the physics push it one way, so it topples. block_art() chooses the plank picture for any size that is much wider than it is tall. The collision shape and the picture always use the same size.

Success criteria. The crate falls, hits the ground and settles. Each press of R gives a different starting angle.

Apply it to your project. In Godot, add a RigidBody2D with a Sprite2D and a CollisionShape2D as children. Run the scene, and it falls with no code. Add a StaticBody2D floor with its own CollisionShape2D for it to land on.
