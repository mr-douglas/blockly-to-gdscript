Physics course · Lesson 3

Throw a ball at a tower

Objective. Aim with the keyboard, launch a rigid body with an impulse, and check when the targets have been knocked out.

Freeze, aim, then push

The green alien is a RigidBody2D with freeze = true, so gravity ignores it while you aim. Up and Down change angle, Left and Right change power. Vector2.from_angle() turns the angle into a direction. Angles in Godot turn clockwise, and y points down the screen, so “up” needs a negative angle.

func launch_velocity() -> Vector2:
	return Vector2.from_angle(deg_to_rad(-angle)) * power
...
ball.freeze = false
ball.apply_central_impulse(launch_velocity() * ball.mass)

An impulse is a sudden push, like a kick. A push of velocity × mass gives any ball exactly that velocity.

Predicting the path

The yellow line uses the equation of motion position = start + velocity × t + ½ × gravity × t² to draw the first 0.55 seconds of the flight. Godot’s engine uses the same rule, so the ball follows the line until it hits something.

A target counts as knocked out once it has moved more than 30 pixels from where it started. set_meta() stores that starting position on each target.

Predict, change, and test

  1. Knock out both aliens. How many shots did it take?
  2. Change ball.mass to 10. The ball leaves at the same speed, so predict whether the tower falls more easily.
  3. Remove * ball.mass from the impulse line and keep the mass at 10. What happens to the shot, and does it still follow the yellow line?
  4. Call build_tower(660) as well, to add a second tower further away.
Check the prediction

A heavier ball at the same speed carries more momentum, so it smashes through the tower. Without * ball.mass, the same push gives a heavy ball a tenth of the speed. It falls short of the yellow line, which assumes the full speed. The second tower adds two more aliens, and the counter changes to “/ 4” because it uses targets.size().

Success criteria. You can aim, throw and knock out every alien, and you can explain why the impulse is multiplied by the mass.

Apply it to your project. Turn the knocked-out count into a score shown in a HUD (see the interface course), and give the player a limited number of shots.
