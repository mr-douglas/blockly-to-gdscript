Physics course · Lesson 2

Build a tower with mass and friction

Objective. Use a function and a loop to build many rigid bodies, then change their mass and friction.

One function, many blocks

Every block needs the same five steps: make the body, place it, set its mass, give it a shape and draw it. add_block() does all five, so the tower is just a short loop. Each time round the loop builds one storey (two posts and a plank) and moves base_y up by 145 pixels for the next one.

for storey in 2:
	add_block(Vector2(330, base_y - 55), Vector2(35, 110), "wood", WOOD_MASS)
	add_block(Vector2(438, base_y - 55), Vector2(35, 110), "wood", WOOD_MASS)
	add_block(Vector2(384, base_y - 127.5), Vector2(160, 35), "wood", WOOD_MASS)
	base_y -= 145

Mass and PhysicsMaterial

mass sets how heavy a body is. Heavy bodies push light ones out of the way. A PhysicsMaterial describes the surface: friction (0 is ice, 1 is very grippy) and bounce (0 is a beanbag, 1 is a superball). Press Space to drop a stone block that is four times heavier than the wood.

Open the editor on its own page

Predict, change, and test

  1. Change for storey in 2 to 3. Where does the third storey appear?
  2. Set STONE_MASS to 0.2 and drop the stone. Then try 20.
  3. Set FRICTION to 0.0. Predict what happens to the tower before you press Space.
  4. Set surface.bounce to 0.8 and drop the stone again.
Check the prediction

A third storey sits on top of the second because base_y keeps going up. A very light stone bounces off the plank, and a very heavy one smashes through. With no friction the planks slide off the posts as soon as anything wobbles. Bouncy blocks make the whole tower jitter and fly apart.

Success criteria. The tower stands until the stone hits it, and you can explain how mass and friction changed what happened.

Apply it to your project. In the Inspector, a RigidBody2D has Mass and a Physics Material Override slot. Create a new PhysicsMaterial there, then save it as a .tres file so many bodies can share it.
