Physics course · Lesson 5

Knock down a crate pyramid in 3D

Objective. Build a 3D aiming direction from two angles, then throw a ball at a pyramid of crates.

Nested loops build the pyramid

The pyramid has three rows. Row 0 has 3 crates, row 1 has 2 and row 2 has 1, so the inner loop runs 3 - row times. A third loop makes it two crates deep. The strong metal-banded crates go on the bottom row.

Aiming in 3D

In 2D one angle was enough. In 3D you need two: how far to tip the throw up (Up and Down) and how far to turn it left or right (Left and Right). The code starts with a direction along x, tips it up, then rotates it around the vertical axis:

var direction := Vector3(cos(up), sin(up), 0)
return direction.rotated(Vector3.UP, deg_to_rad(turn))

The yellow arrow shows that direction. Space unfreezes the ball and gives it an impulse along the arrow, just like the 2D lesson.

Predict, change, and test

  1. Knock down all 12 crates. Which angle works best?
  2. Change for row in 3 to 4 and 3 - row to 4 - row. How many crates are there now?
  3. Change SPEED to 6. Which angle do you need now?
  4. Change Vector3.UP to Vector3.RIGHT in launch_direction(), then press Left and Right. What happens to the arrow?
Check the prediction

Four rows two deep gives (4 + 3 + 2 + 1) × 2 = 20 crates. A slower ball needs a higher angle to reach the same distance. Rotating around Vector3.RIGHT (the x axis) rolls the arrow around itself instead of turning it, so Left and Right only tilt it sideways a little. The axis you rotate around matters.

Success criteria. You can aim in 3D and knock the pyramid down, and you can explain how the two angles make one direction.

Apply it to your project. Swap the red ball for your own imported model. Give the player three balls, and use a Label in a CanvasLayer to show how many crates are left.
