Pan and zoom to keep both cars visible

Objective. Centre a fixed-angle camera between two players and choose a zoom that fits both.

Calculate the midpoint and the required view

The camera still looks straight down. Move its X and Z to the midpoint between the cars. With KEEP_HEIGHT, orthographic size is the height of the view, and the width is height times the viewport aspect ratio. Convert the needed width into a height before choosing the larger requirement.

var height_for_depth := span.z + 2.0 * FRAME_MARGIN
var height_for_width := (span.x + 2.0 * FRAME_MARGIN) / maxf(0.01, aspect)
race_camera.size = maxf(MIN_VIEW, maxf(height_for_depth, height_for_width))

Predict, change, and test

  1. Let the cars get close, then drive one away. Predict which way the centre moves and when the camera zooms out.
  2. Set FRAME_MARGIN to 3.0. Compare how much road is visible without changing either car’s position.
  3. Temporarily use only height_for_depth in the size calculation. Spread the cars across the track in X. Explain why a car can be clipped, then restore the width check.
Check the prediction

The centre moves halfway towards the moving car. The camera zooms out when either separation needs a larger view. A larger margin gives more room around both cars. Ignoring the width can lose a car that is far to the left or right.

Success criteria. Both cars stay in view when close, far apart, side by side, or at opposite ends of the track. The angle stays fixed.

Apply it to your project. Use the same calculation for two objects in another shared-screen game. Test wide and tall windows, and include room for the objects’ actual size.
