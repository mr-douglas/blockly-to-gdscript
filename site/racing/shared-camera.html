Pan and zoom to keep both cars visible

Objective. Centre a fixed-angle camera between two players and choose a distance that fits both.

Calculate the midpoint and the required view

The camera keeps the same downward tilt, PITCH_DEGREES, and aims at the midpoint between the cars. A perspective camera zooms by moving closer or further away. At a distance d, the view is 2 * d * tan(fov / 2) high, and its width is that height times the viewport aspect ratio. Because the camera is tilted, it sees the gap between the cars in Z shortened by sin(pitch). Work out the height each gap needs, use the larger one, and turn that height into a distance.

var height_for_depth := span.z * sin(pitch) + 2.0 * FRAME_MARGIN
var height_for_width := (span.x + 2.0 * FRAME_MARGIN) / maxf(0.01, aspect)
var view_height := maxf(height_for_depth, height_for_width)
var distance := view_height / (2.0 * tan(deg_to_rad(race_camera.fov) / 2.0))

The camera then glides towards its target with lerp(), so the view follows smoothly instead of jumping.

Predict, change, and test

  1. Let the cars get close, then drive one away. Predict which way the centre moves and when the camera pulls back.
  2. Set FRAME_MARGIN to 3.0. Compare how much road is visible without changing either car’s position.
  3. Temporarily use only height_for_depth for view_height. Spread the cars across the track in X. Explain why a car can be clipped, then restore the width check.
  4. Change PITCH_DEGREES to 30 and then 80. Describe how the angle changes what you can see.
Check the prediction

The centre moves halfway towards the moving car. The camera pulls back when either gap needs a larger view. A larger margin gives more room around both cars. Ignoring the width can lose a car that is far to the left or right. A low pitch shows more scenery towards the horizon. A high pitch looks almost straight down.

Success criteria. Both cars stay in view when close, far apart, side by side, or at opposite ends of the track. The angle stays fixed.

Apply it to your project. Use the same calculation for two objects in another shared-screen game. Test wide and tall windows, and include room for the objects’ actual size.
