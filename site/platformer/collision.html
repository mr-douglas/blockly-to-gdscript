Make walls solid

Objective. Use collision shapes and move_and_slide() to move without passing through walls.

Give both bodies a shape

The wall is a StaticBody2D. The player is a CharacterBody2D. Each needs a CollisionShape2D. The grass blocks and the frog show where they are. The collision shapes tell Godot which space is solid. Set velocity.x in pixels per second. move_and_slide() uses the physics tick internally.

func _physics_process(_delta: float) -> void:
    var direction := Input.get_axis("left", "right")
    player.velocity.x = direction * SPEED
    player.move_and_slide()

Predict, change, and test

  1. Hold Right until the player reaches the wall. Predict what happens if you keep holding the key.
  2. Temporarily replace player.move_and_slide() with player.position.x += player.velocity.x * _delta. Test the same wall, then restore the call.
  3. Increase the width of the player’s add_box() shape. Predict where the frog will stop and test it.
Check the prediction

The player stops at the wall when using move_and_slide(). Setting position directly bypasses that collision check. A wider collision shape makes the frog stop farther from the wall.

Success criteria. The player stops at either wall and can move away. You can explain why a visible picture alone does not make an object solid.

Apply it to your project. Give the player and wall matching rectangle collision shapes in your editor scene. Replace direct position updates with velocity and move_and_slide(). Test a wall in each direction.
