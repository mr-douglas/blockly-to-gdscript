Two particles collide on a rough slope

Cambridge 9709/45, Summer 2026, Question 6. Take g = 10 m/s² and choose down the plane as positive.

P (0.3 kg) starts 2.75 m uphill of Q (0.6 kg). For the plane, sin α = 0.28 and μ = 0.5. After the initial release, P is projected towards Q at 6 m/s. It rebounds at 1 m/s immediately after contact.

Work through the question

(a) Why do both stay at rest?

Choose Release from rest. For each mass, compare the downhill component of weight with the maximum static friction. Predict the actual friction.

Show part (a)

cos α = 0.96. For either mass m, the downhill weight component is 2.8m N and R = 9.6m N. The limit μR is 4.8m N, greater than the force needed to hold either particle. Actual friction acts uphill with magnitude 0.84 N on P and 1.68 N on Q.

(b) How fast is P before contact?

Predict P’s acceleration while sliding downhill. Use its starting speed and the 2.75 m displacement, then check Just before impact.

Show part (b)

While sliding down, friction is 4.8m N up the plane. Resolving down gives a = 2.8 − 4.8 = −2 m/s². From v² = 6² + 2(−2)(2.75) = 25, P’s impact speed is 5 m/s. Q is still at rest.

(c) How far apart do they stop?

Compare Just before impact and Just after impact. Why does P’s friction change sign? Find Q’s outgoing velocity, then calculate the stopping distance of each particle.

Show part (c)

Conserve momentum along the plane during the brief impact. Taking P’s rebound as −1 m/s, 0.3(5) + 0.6(0) = 0.3(−1) + 0.6vQ. So Q moves down at 3 m/s.

P moves uphill, so both gravity’s parallel component and friction act downhill. Its signed acceleration is +7.6 m/s² and it travels 1²/(2 × 7.6) = 5/76 m uphill. Q moves downhill with acceleration −2 m/s² and travels 3²/(2 × 2) = 2.25 m downhill. The particles move in opposite directions from the contact point, so add the distances. Their separation is 44/19 m ≈ 2.32 m.
