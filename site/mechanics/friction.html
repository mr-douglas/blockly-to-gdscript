Will the block slide?

Objective. Decide whether a particle stays at rest, is in limiting equilibrium, or slides down a rough slope.

The particle starts from rest. Use g = 10 m/s². The model uses the same coefficient μ for the limiting value and for sliding. Orange shows weight, blue shows the normal reaction, and yellow shows friction acting up the plane.

Predict, then test

  1. For 2 kg on a 30° slope with μ = 0.30, calculate R, μR and the down-slope component of weight. Will the block slide? If so, find its acceleration.
  2. Increase μ to 0.70. Predict the actual friction needed to keep the block at rest. Compare it with the maximum μR.
  3. Set the angle to 45° and μ to 1. Explain why this is limiting equilibrium. Lower μ slightly and test again.

Check your working

Show the equations and answers

Find R = mg cos θ. At rest, friction balances mg sin θ as long as that force is at most μR. At the limit they are equal. If the driving force exceeds the limit, friction is μR in this model and ma = mg sin θ − μR.

At 30° and μ = 0.30, R ≈ 17.32 N and friction ≈ 5.20 N. The down-slope force is 10 N, giving acceleration ≈ 2.40 m/s². With μ = 0.70, actual friction is 10 N, below the limit of about 12.12 N. At 45° and μ = 1, sin 45° = cos 45°, so both parallel forces have equal magnitude and friction is at its limit.

Try the equation in GDScript

Predict the result for μ = 0.70, then change the coefficient. Explain why the maxf line returns zero instead of an acceleration up the plane.

var angle = deg_to_rad(30.0)
var coefficient = 0.30
var acceleration = maxf(0.0, 10.0 * (sin(angle) - coefficient * cos(angle)))
print(snappedf(acceleration, 0.001))
